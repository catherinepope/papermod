Tagging is an easy method of categorising or labelling your Zotero items by adding keywords. These tags allow you to filter your items in a variety of ways through the Tag Selector in the bottom left-hand corner of Zotero.

A good way of identifying and easily locating your most important tags is to colour-code them. For instance, I have an orange `tbr` tag, which means 'to be read' and a green `borrow` tag for books I need to retrieve from the library.

{{< img src="images/zotero-tag-selector.jpg" alt="Screenshot of Zotero tag selector" center="true" >}}

To colour-code a tag, right-click it in the Tag Selector and choose **Assign Colour**.

{{< img src="images/zotero-assign-colour.jpg" alt="Screenshot of Zotero tag selector" center="true" >}}

Pick a colour from the swatch and click **Set Colour**. You can do this for up to nine tags.

{{< img src="images/zotero-set-colour.jpg" alt="Screenshot of Zotero tag selector" center="true" >}}

Any items with coloured tags assigned to them are flagged with a matching blob in the middle column. It's then easy to see at a glance what items I need to borrow or read.

{{< img src="images/zotero-coloured-blobs.jpg" alt="Screenshot of Zotero coloured blobs" center="true" >}}

In the colour-coding settings, you can see that my `tbr` tag has also been assigned a numerical keyboard shortcut of `1`. This means I can assign this tag to another item by selecting it and pressing 1. To remove it, just click `1` again. This a great time-saver for tags you use most frequently. 

{{< img src="images/zotero-assign-tag-number.jpg" alt="Screenshot of Zotero tag numbering" center="true" >}}

There’s no right way to use these colours. You could also use them to visually flag different themes, or to indicate the importance of a source. Tags are helpful for applying [the Bacon Method](/posts/reading-strategically-with-the-bacon-method/) that I explained in an earlier post. It’s a shame there are only nine colours available, but at least that prevents us from creating overly complicated systems rather than getting on with our writing 😳
